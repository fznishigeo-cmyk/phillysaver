Deal hacks

Fourteen tactics we would tell a friend. None of them involve a coupon-code generator or a second account.

1.Read the strike-through like a skeptic

A crossed-out price is a claim, not a fact. Ask three things: was that price ever charged, was it charged recently, and was it charged by this seller? If the listing cannot answer, treat the "was" price as decoration and judge the deal on the real price alone.

2.Check the price history before you trust the percent

Free price-history tools track what an item has actually sold for over months. A "60% off" that sits right at the item's normal low is a normal day, not a deal. A price under the all-time low is the real thing. We do this check for our "price history" badge; you can do it for anything.

3.Shop open-box, then mind the return window

Warehouse and open-box listings are where the deepest discounts hide. The item is usually fine; the box took the hit. Read the condition grade, confirm what is in the box, and note the return window, which is often shorter than for new stock. Test the item the day it arrives, not the week after.

4.Do the coupon and Subscribe & Save math by hand

Clip coupons stack with subscription discounts on some marketplaces, and the combined price is not always shown until checkout. Add it up yourself: base price, minus the coupon, minus the subscription percent. Then cancel the subscription after the first delivery if you do not want a second one.

5.Learn the price-match policy before you need it

Many big retailers will match a lower price from a short list of competitors, and some will match their own online price in store. The rules live on a policy page: which stores count, whether marketplace sellers count, and how long after purchase you can claim it. Read it once; it pays for years.

6.Let the cart sit

Add the item, make sure you are logged in, then close the tab. Some stores send a reminder email within a day or two, and some of those include a discount. It does not always work, and it costs nothing to try. Just do not do it on a deal that will be gone in an hour.

7.Stack a cashback portal with a rewards card

Start your purchase from a cashback portal, pay with a card that earns rewards on that category, and you have two small discounts that stack on top of the sale price. Check the portal rate right before you click; rates change often and some exclude gift cards or certain departments.

8.Buy the gift card at a discount first

Discounted gift cards from reputable resellers, warehouse clubs, or card-issuer reward programs turn a sale price into a sale price minus a few percent more. Buy only from sellers with a guarantee, and use the card soon rather than holding a balance for months.

9.Keep a clearance calendar

Stores clear space on a schedule. Outdoor gear and grills get marked down as fall arrives, holiday decor the week after the holiday, school supplies in September, and last year's TVs right before the new models ship in spring. Write down the dates that matter to you and shop then, not when the ad says so.

10.Click every color and size

One listing can hide a dozen prices. The popular color sells at full price while the odd one sits on clearance. The size nobody wants is half off. If you can live with teal, teal might be the deal. Check every variant before you decide the item is not on sale.

11.Take refurbished seriously, with a warranty

A refurbished item from the manufacturer or a certified program is tested, repaired if needed, and usually comes with a warranty. That warranty is the whole point. Refurbished with a warranty is a smart buy; refurbished from a random seller with no warranty is a used item with a nicer name.

12.Treat "limit per customer" as a signal

When a store limits how many you can buy, it is usually because the price is low enough that resellers would clear the shelf. That is a good sign for you. Buy the limit if you will use it, and do not try to beat the limit; it is not worth losing an account over.

13.Buy the thing in its off-season

The best price on a space heater is in April. The best price on a fan is in October. The best price on a snow shovel is roughly never, except the week the weather turns warm. If you can store it, buying six months early is the easiest discount there is.

14.Set a walk-away price, then walk away

Before you shop, decide what the item is worth to you, in dollars, and write it down. If the price is under that number, buy without agonizing. If it is over, close the tab. This one rule beats every countdown timer, every "only 2 left," and every crossed-out price ever printed.

One more thing

Most of these take a minute. The only one that takes real discipline is the last one, and it is the one that saves the most money. The wall at wowpriced is built around the same idea: a deal is a real price that is far enough under a real price. Everything else is a sign on an end cap.
